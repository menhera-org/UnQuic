What a daemon pays when its sockets are not on the outer link itself, November 26.
Profile build of 836057a, relay bed, two workers per daemon, 7.5 ms round trip, no loss,
sixteen QinQ flows at 2 Gbit/s each way, two 20 s points (the second is listed).
OUTER_WIRE=1: the relay's side of each link segments its trains, so datagrams cross the
link one by one and the endpoint's coalescing decides what the daemon receives.
Cycles are the left daemon's, per datagram sent (7.35-7.40 million a run, and as many
received). All runs deliver 1.97 Gbit/s each way.

Without OUTER_WIRE a veth hands the relay's trains over whole and all shapes cost the
same (66-70% and 62-65%, socket read 760-780 cycles a datagram), which no card does.

Not in these numbers: the kernel's own work to receive, route and deliver each
datagram. On this bed it runs on the relay's cores. On a box it runs on the box's
cores, once a datagram without forwarded coalescing and once a train with it.
